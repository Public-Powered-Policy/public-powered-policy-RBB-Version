Former Justice Department Civil Rights Lawyers Launch Civic Education Group to Help Americans Shape Federal Rules

New podcast, “Regularly Speaking,” explains federal rulemaking in everyday language and shows listeners how to participate

Contact: media@publicpoweredpolicy.org

October 27, 2026

BETHESDA, Md.— Former U.S. Department of Justice civil rights lawyers today launched Public Powered Policy, a nonpartisan civic education organization designed to make the federal rulemaking process easier to understand and use. As part of this mission, Public Powered Policy also launches its new podcast called “Regularly Speaking” that explains federal rules in everyday language and shows listeners how they can participate. The first episode is available today.

“Voting is one way to shape federal policy. Public comment is another, and it continues after the polls close,” said Rebecca Bond, co-founder and executive director of Public Powered Policy. “You don't need to be a lawyer to comment. You're the expert in your own life, and that is the information agencies need.”

Federal agencies write rules that affect everything from the water coming out of the kitchen faucet to the safety of school lunches and the ads that appear on our phones. Before many of those rules become final, the public has an opportunity to comment. Agencies are required by law to consider the comments they receive and respond to relevant and significant comments when issuing a final rule.

Public Powered Policy's goal is to educate the public on important rules being proposed and how to submit comments, so that federal rulemaking reflects a broader range of experiences and needs from communities across the country.

Bond is launching Public Powered Policy after spending 25 years in the Civil Rights Division of the U.S. Department of Justice, including 12 as chief of the Disability Rights Section. She oversaw major Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) rules on web accessibility for state and local governments, movie captioning, and accessible medical diagnostic equipment.

“When we wrote the medical diagnostic equipment rule, people with disabilities told us they couldn't be accurately weighed at the doctor's office, even though medications such as anesthesia are dosed based on weight,” Bond said. “Those comments gave us insights we wouldn't otherwise have had. Individual stories can teach policymakers how proposed rules would work in the real world.”

Kristin Stitcher is the co-founder and director of policy and programs at Public Powered Policy, and worked with Bond on ADA regulations at the Justice Department.

“The people who write federal rules can't see how a rule will play out in every person's daily life,” Stitcher said. “Public comments fill that gap. The process is open to everyone, but most people don't know it exists. We want to help explain what it is, how to use it, and why your experiences are important to share.”

Bond and Stitcher are co-hosts of Public Powered Policy's podcast, “Regularly Speaking.” The first episode, available today, looks at an early-stage rulemaking from the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). The agency is asking what responsibility online ad platforms should have when scammers use their tools to impersonate banks, businesses, and government agencies. In 2025, the FTC received more than 1 million reports of imposter scams, with nearly $3.5 billion in reported losses. Public comments are due November 30.

Public Powered Policy does not take positions on proposed rules. Each episode of “Regularly Speaking” explains what an agency is proposing, who might be affected, and how members of the public can submit comments if they choose to participate. Episodes also answer common questions about rulemaking and offer practical tips for writing comments that agencies can use.

“Regularly Speaking” is available on Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab), Spotify (opens in a new tab) and YouTube (opens in a new tab), with new episodes released every other week. Plain-language guides to the public-comment process are available at publicpoweredpolicy.org.

About Public Powered Policy

Public Powered Policy is a nonpartisan civic education organization that helps people understand and participate in the federal rulemaking process. It was founded by Rebecca Bond, Kristin Stitcher and Cameron Lynch, who worked together in the U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division. Public Powered Policy is a fiscally sponsored project of Social Good Fund, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

Learn more at publicpoweredpolicy.org.
